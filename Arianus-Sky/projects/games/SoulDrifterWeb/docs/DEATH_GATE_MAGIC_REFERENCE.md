# Death Gate Cycle Magic Reference and SoulDrifter Translation

Purpose: factual inspiration reference, not shipping canon  
Original novels: Margaret Weis and Tracy Hickman, 1990–1994

## What the books actually distinguish

### The Wave and rune magic

The setting treats reality as a field of possible outcomes held in balance by the Wave. Sartan and Patryn rune magic is the highest-order magic because it selects and strengthens a possible outcome. A likely alteration is easier than an extremely improbable one. The runes are a grammar for changing probability, not merely elemental spell symbols.

### Sartan method

Sartan form rune structures through prescribed movement, gesture, song, spoken tones, and runes drawn in air or on a surface. They can also inscribe permanent enchantments into objects and places. Their magic is culturally communal and formal.

### Patryn method

Patryn carry extensive rune structures tattooed on their bodies and activate or extend them through voice and runes drawn in air or on surfaces. Their approach is fast, personal, survival-oriented, and physically integrated. Patryn are not uniformly evil; the series gives them individual loyalties, moral choices, and reasons shaped by the Labyrinth.

### Human Spiritual Masteries

Human magic works directly on the mental, emotional, and spiritual relationship between caster and world. Described capabilities include elemental manipulation, illusion, healing, compulsion, transformation, translocation, and overcoming physical limits such as gravity. Most casters use speech, gesture, objects, or incantation.

### Elven Physical Masteries

Elven magic primarily enhances physical objects and mechanisms: guided arrows, flying ships, magical submersibles, protective charms, hardened fabric, and other enchanted technology. Magic can also be woven into songs and triggered by a singer. The Kenkari are a specialized exception using spirit communication, soul containment, and transformation.

### Dwarves

Dwarves have no comparable innate spellcasting tradition in the novels. Some can trigger Sartan-created songs, runes, or enchanted amulets without authoring the underlying high magic themselves.

### Necromancy

Necromancy is a forbidden Sartan practice developed on Abarrach to raise the dead. Its central balance cost is severe: keeping one soul from passing causes another life to end prematurely. The practice contributes to plague, loss of control, and dangerous undead states. It is an application of higher magic with a moral/metaphysical price, not a harmless low-level school.

### Dragons, dragon-snakes, gates, and nullification

The good dragons and evil dragon-snakes are metaphysical beings connected to moral balance rather than ordinary learnable schools. The Death Gate, Seventh Gate, world-changing structures, and Colossi are vast applications of rune magic. Chelestra's special water suppresses Sartan/Patryn rune magic, demonstrating that even high magic has hard counters.

## Important correction for our design vocabulary

`Void magic` and `Dark magic` are not clean standalone school categories in the Death Gate novels. They are SoulDrifter additions. `Death magic` maps loosely to the books' necromancy but SoulDrifter must define its own costs, creatures, spells, and metaphysics.

Likewise, SoulDrifter does not ship the proper names Sartan, Patryn, Death Gate, Abarrach, the Wave, or the book series' named characters and factions. Our production terms are Aether-Scribed, Flesh-Forged, possibility weaving, Fire Realm, Worldcoil Serpents, and the setting-specific names defined in the game bible.
