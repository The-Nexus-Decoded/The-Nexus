# SoulDrifter Humanoid Facial Fit Pipeline

Status: mandatory production contract for issue #487 and every later compatible adult humanoid head

## Purpose

Fit one proven facial-animation system to differently sized and shaped approved Smart Mesh heads without replacing their visible identity or inventing a new control scheme per character.

The reusable unit is the pipeline and its standardized output contract. The fitted landmarks and baked deformations remain head-specific. A Human, Elf, Dwarf, or later compatible adult humanoid may reuse the same procedure and animation semantics, but it never reuses another head's unvalidated vertex deltas, seam, corrective shapes, or final facial asset.

## Locked architecture

- The exact approved Tripo Quad Smart Mesh head is the visible `Basis` and neutral identity authority.
- The existing body-owned Mixamo 65-bone armature remains the runtime body skeleton.
- Blender 5.2.1 LTS Rigify 0.6.10 is the temporary facial authoring and correction rig.
- Use Rigify `face.skin_eye`, `face.skin_jaw`, and `face.basic_tongue`, plus their required `skin.stretchy_chain` support.
- Never generate Rigify into, splice its generated bones into, or replace the accepted Mixamo armature. `META-*` and `RIG-*` facial objects are authoring-only and must be stripped before runtime export.
- Author facial motion directly on a head-local duplicate of the exact approved head with the fitted Rigify controls, then bake evaluated positions back to shape keys on the untouched exact `Basis`. Cross-topology vertex-delta transfer is not a production path.
- Runtime facial expressions use the complete 52-name ARKit/MediaPipe-compatible blendshape vocabulary.
- Runtime speech uses the 15-name Meta-style semantic vocabulary. `viseme_sil` is a zero-weight runtime state; the other 14 targets are authored and corrected as direct baked shapes that each contain their complete jaw, lip, tooth, and tongue deformation. Never layer `jawOpen`, `mouthClose`, or another ARKit mouth target over a direct speech viseme.
- Adult age and facial-identity variants remain separate topology-preserving morph layers. Skin tone, hair color, and greying remain material parameters. Hair and facial hair remain separate modular geometry.

Epic, Faceware, and real-actor footage provide performance and deformation references. They are validation evidence, not replacement head geometry or a second runtime rig.

Pixel-generating video systems, including MiniMax H3 and MiniMax Design, are optional performance-reference tools only. Their rendered frames and audio do not prove or supply target-head topology, bones, Rigify controls, blendshapes, viseme weights, animation curves, or a runtime GLB. A selected character card or visually consistent generated clip is still generative pixel continuity rather than exact mesh continuity. Never promote, retarget, or ship one as facial-animation data. If such a service is used for a reference clip, separately receipt its model/version, prompt, inputs, output, commercial-use terms, and generated-content disclosure. MiniMax H3 open weights must not be run from an excluded territory under the current Community License; hosted MiniMax Design/API terms are a separate review.

## Repeatable per-head procedure

### 1. Lock the incoming identity

Record and hash:

- accepted body and head asset IDs;
- exact untouched Smart Mesh and extracted-head files;
- body type, scale, axes, origin, and versioned neck seam;
- vertex, face, UV, material, normal, and skin-weight signatures;
- the 65-bone armature hierarchy, root, rest transforms, and head/neck bones; and
- approved neutral front, profile, and three-quarter renders.

Reject a head from a merely similar body. Reject any procedure that changes the neutral identity before a facial target is applied.

### 2. Build one deterministic exact-head semantic fit

Create a versioned landmark, region, and fit record entirely on the exact target head. At minimum it contains:

- hard pins: every neck-seam vertex, mouth corners, upper/lower lip centers, eye inner/outer corners, complete ordered eyelid curves, nose tip and alar points, and chin;
- curves: outer and inner lips, brows, and jawline;
- soft landmarks: cheeks, temples, forehead, and jaw angles;
- helpers: eyeball centers/radii, upper/lower incisors, tongue root, and tongue tip;
- semantic regions that prevent a sample from crossing upper/lower lip, left/right side, eyelid/eyeball, mouth cavity, or neck-seam boundaries; and
- target vertex or logical-coordinate IDs, ordered boundary membership, skin-weight region, symmetry partner, and measured target-head position.

Fit Rigify's sample landmarks to these measured exact-head landmarks and ordered curves. Derive every translation and scale from receipt-locked target and sample measurements; unexplained anatomical offsets or generic per-head constants are prohibited. Broad distance masks, Gaussian whole-face deltas, nearest-delta copying, and cross-topology remapping are prohibited.

Every semantic skinning and corrective region must be topology/geodesic rather than an axis-aligned coordinate window. Eye regions include ordered eyelid rings plus brow, socket, and cheek depth anchors; aperture landmarks alone are not a valid 3D fit. Jaw regions include ordered outer and inner lip boundaries, recessed upper/lower mouth sheets, chin, jaw angles, and a measured hinge/pivot. Derive and receipt-lock anatomical measurements once, then reuse them in metarig placement, binding, baking, and acceptance checks.

Issue #487 permanently rejected the unconstrained global RBF route after the locked proof produced a negative local Jacobian determinant for `eyeBlinkRight` (`-4.0207249023661084e-05`) and the local similarity fallback missed eye landmarks by roughly 7.6-8.1 mm and the jaw by roughly 35.0 mm. The failure receipt SHA-256 is `BE4C8A035FFC968BAD6252D0FC653D2A59FFEDDED95F2F09ECCA94D38E948A90`. Do not revive that route by widening tolerances or hiding failed evidence.

Before baking a target, validate that the fitted Rigify module generates cleanly, binds only to its measured target region, leaves the full region boundary and neck seam unchanged, and produces no triangle inversion, nonadjacent self-intersection, duplicate-vertex split, or opposite-side motion. Failure invalidates the fit or weights rather than widening a tolerance.

### 3. Fit the temporary Rigify authoring rig

- Place `face.skin_eye` roots at measured eyeball centers with optical-axis vectors, then sample the exact ordered upper/lower eyelid curves into the required mirrored connected chains.
- Place `face.skin_jaw` and its four upper/lower, left/right lip chains from the explicit ordered lip curves; add independent upper/lower teeth helpers.
- Place the connected `face.basic_tongue` chain from measured tongue root through tip.
- Keep the generated Rigify rig separate from the Mixamo armature. Preview-parent its face root to `mixamorig:Head` with a rest-preserving constraint only for authoring playback.
- Hash the metarig placement/correspondence receipt so the same head can be regenerated deterministically.

### 4. Author and correct standardized targets directly

For each of the 52 exact ARKit-compatible target names:

1. duplicate the untouched exact target `Basis` in head-local space for authoring only;
2. drive the fitted Rigify eye, jaw, lip, tooth, or tongue controls according to the recorded real-performance mechanics for that target;
3. constrain binding to the measured exact-head semantic region and keep its boundary, opposite side, unaffected regions, and full neck seam fixed;
4. add head-specific corrective sculpting through the fitted controls without changing neutral identity;
5. keep coordinate-duplicate vertices identical and reject folds, tears, collisions, and implausible contacts;
6. bake the evaluated exact-head positions back onto the untouched canonical mesh under the exact standardized name; and
7. record the controls, weights, values, frame, reference time range, and resulting packed-float target hash so the shape can be regenerated deterministically.

Repeat the same exact-head control, corrective, and bake procedure for the 14 non-silent `visemes02` names. Correct and bake them directly under their exact names. The source `viseme_sil.target` is empty by design, so silence is represented by zeroing the 14 speech weights and is not required as runtime geometry. Do not synthesize speech by layering ARKit jaw or mouth shapes over these direct visemes, and do not rename a pose to a standard target without proving its mechanics and contacts against the reference packet.

### 5. Preserve independent creator layers

- `YOUNG_ADULT`, `MIDDLE_AGED`, and `ELDER` are separate age layers on the same vertex order and seam.
- Soft/round, angular/high-cheek, broad/strong, and later creator feature controls are separate identity layers on the same topology.
- Skin tone is independent of facial geometry.
- Hair and facial hair are separately toggleable meshes and must be fitted against the complete accepted age/identity envelope, not neutral alone.
- Children use a separate NPC-only body/head/rig family. Never shrink or age-morph this adult head into a child.

### 5A. Fail-closed creator presentation and modular-appearance gate

- Keep `Body Type` and `Face & Features` as separate creator stages. The body stage shows the complete accepted body in its rig-inspection pose with every extremity inside frame. The face stage switches to a readable head-and-shoulders close-up.
- Use a stable relaxed Human idle in the face stage: feet planted, head and gaze substantially stable, subtle chest expansion and shoulder response, and no repeated nodding, listening gesture, fidget, or locomotion. An NPC-listen animation is not a creator idle.
- When an accepted stock idle contains usable breathing but repeated neck/head motion, derive a creator-only clip by holding every neck/head/head-end position and rotation track at its neutral first frame while preserving the authored spine and shoulder tracks. Receipt and regression-test the derived clip; do not overwrite the gameplay source animation.
- Automatic model rotation is opt-in and defaults off. Provide a deterministic front-view reset plus deliberate drag/orbit inspection so the owner can hold a facial feature still while comparing it.
- Fail closed in the UI. Do not expose an age, identity, hair, facial-hair, freckle, or material option merely because a control or label exists. Enable it only after the corresponding runtime artifact, receipt, fresh-import proof, and visual gate pass. A disabled control must state why it is unavailable.
- Hair color and hair greying remain disabled until the selected hairstyle has visible approved geometry. Facial-hair greying remains disabled for `none` and for every unapproved facial-hair module. A control that changes stored state but has no visible accepted target is a failed gate, not a partial implementation.
- Hair and facial hair are authored offline as separate modular runtime geometry fitted to the exact canonical head. They are not generated on the player device, fused into the head, or approximated with a generic rigid cap.
- Author the hairstyle family per canonical race/head topology, not per class. Classes share the same scalp. Reuse the source groom across compatible face/age morphs; add explicit neck/shoulder corrective fits only where a body type changes the collision envelope of a medium or long style.
- For groom-based styles, use Blender's documented surface-attached workflow: bind guide roots to the exact head and its UV map with [Attach Hair Curves to Surface](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/modeling/geometry_nodes/hair/utility/attach_hair_curves_to_surface.html), record the rest-surface attachment, propagate the accepted guide design with [Interpolate Hair Curves](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/modeling/geometry_nodes/hair/generation/interpolate_hair_curves.html), and use [Shrinkwrap Hair Curves](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/modeling/geometry_nodes/hair/deformation/shrinkwrap_hair_curves.html) with locked roots and explicit positive clearance where conformance is required. Bake or convert to the runtime strand/card mesh only after this exact-head groom passes; receipt the head/UV hash, guide and root counts, offsets, deformation path, and conversion settings.
- Explicitly bind the attachment node to the receipt-locked scalp UV attribute and provide a point-domain rest-position attribute with resting-surface evaluation enabled. Validate actual root-UV diversity and prove that roots remain stationary through accepted face/age deformation; the mere existence of a `UVMap` attribute is not an attachment proof.
- Blender's Interpolate Curves `Points` input is the final child-root point set; the node blends curve shape from nearby guides but does not scatter roots. Never feed it tiny jitter copies of a polar/ring guide layout. Preserve the accepted guides as flow donors, then build an independent deterministic blue-noise or stratified scalp-surface point set for render roots, surface-project it, enforce family-domain and hairline rules, and use three-dimensional minimum separation. Wire the receipt-locked `surface_normal` attribute into both `Guide Up` and `Point Up` so side/rear children do not inherit a constant-world-up twist.
- Receipt guide preservation and child-root distribution as separate gates. Child proof must include exact/unique counts, nearest-neighbor separation, zero exact guide-root reuse within tolerance, donor-distance percentiles/max, scalp clearance, azimuth/vertical occupancy, and a vertical-band-variance test. Coarse bin occupancy alone cannot approve a groom because regular rings can populate every bin while still rendering as comb rows.
- Use Blender 5.2.1's bundled CC0 `procedural_hair_node_assets.blend` as the canonical no-cost groom engine rather than replacing its hair mechanics with bespoke cap-generating math. The issue #487 cache SHA-256 is `B69D8BD8975B9DB7F89693F8F19DD7FCB241F5A3786D2EDFB0A243E91C1109BB`; approved asset groups include Generate, Attach, Interpolate, Clump, Curl, Frizz, Noise, Shrinkwrap, Smooth, Roll, Braid, and Set Hair Curve Profile. Receipt the exact library hash and every node setting used by a hairstyle.
- Retain the surface-attached curves as the editable canonical source, but ship layered textured hair cards through glTF. In the current MSAA-enabled, non-TAA Three.js renderers, use alpha-tested plus alpha-to-coverage, depth-writing, double-sided, non-metallic materials with controlled roughness and anisotropy when supported; do not use grain-producing alpha hashing unless a future temporal resolve is proven. Preserve root/flow/strand breakup through tint and greying. Produce creator/close-up, gameplay, and distance LODs rather than shipping full groom density everywhere.
- Build and receipt card layers separately: a broad low-detail `coverage` layer that hides the scalp, a `mid` layer that establishes the hairstyle volume, a higher-detail `top` layer that carries visible clumps, sparse `flyaways` that break the silhouette, and a dedicated `short-hairs` layer for the hairline and transition zones. A single crossed-card or continuous dome bake is prohibited because it produces the helmet/cap failure seen in the rejected issue #487 candidates.
- Bake a varied clump atlas rather than repeating one fiber mask across every card; receipt each tile, deterministic tile assignment, and atlas occupancy. Reserve crossed cards for buried coverage where they cannot create a starburst or feather silhouette. Hairline, top, and hero clumps use directionally fitted single or curved cards with physically plausible width, overlap, and taper.
- Convert dense child curves into spatially and directionally coherent clump cards rather than one subpixel ribbon per curve or sequential list-order pairs. Use the scalp position, tangent flow, and length to cluster cards; resample members by normalized arc length; and size every cross-section from the full member-curve envelope plus deliberate neighbor overlap. Buried coverage cards use the broadest clusters and retain nearly full width at the root, mid/top cards use progressively smaller clusters, and only short edge hairs or flyaways may taper to a narrow root.
- Treat card geometry proof and shipping-material proof as separate mandatory gates. A solid Workbench render can prove card placement but cannot approve alpha-tested hair. Before visual review, receipt root/body/tip alpha occupancy at the runtime cutoff, connected opaque strand length, 2x/4x/8x mip survival, card-envelope containment, neighboring coverage-footprint overlap, and exact exported glTF/runtime material parity. The authored albedo and roughness atlas must share the generated card UV layout; copying a provider skin or hair image onto unrelated generated UVs is prohibited.
- Generate a root-density or follicle distance-field mask from the accepted groom and blend it into the existing topology-native scalp material as the card-root undercoat. It must tint and grey with the selected hairstyle, preserve ordinary skin outside the scalp region, and switch off with the no-hair state. This is a scalp-material blend, not permission to add a duplicate scalp shell, opaque dome, cap, or helmet mesh.
- A follicle mask is graded root-shadow data, not a binary scalp-region fill. Reject any bake whose UV islands are substantially solid, whose hairline ends as a hard painted band, or whose undercoat alone reads as the hairstyle's volume. Receipt mask histogram/occupancy, hairline falloff, maximum runtime blend, and creator-distance proof with the cards temporarily hidden; this proof must read as subtle follicles or close stubble on ordinary skin, never as a bald cap.
- Bake follicle density in scalp-surface space and rasterize it into every corresponding UV triangle with explicit island-edge padding, so duplicate seam vertices receive matching density from the same three-dimensional neighborhood. A visible UV crack, white lane, or density discontinuity at any scalp seam is a hard rejection even when root-to-surface distance and non-scalp contamination checks pass.
- Render the exact shipping material from at least twelve scalp views at creator distance and actual gameplay distance. Reject exposed scalp lanes or any large connected bald region, repeated dotted/needle/teeth patterns, a ruler-straight hairline, or a card that survives only in geometry-only proof. Prove dark, light/blond, and grey tints before promotion; all must preserve clump detail at the same alpha cutoff and mip levels.
- Treat stubble as a fitted skin-detail layer rather than a miniature full beard: author its density through topology-stable UV albedo, roughness, and normal masks, add only sparse very-short close-up fibers where they materially improve the silhouette, and keep the nose, eyelids, lips, and upper cheek clean. A dense field of black needle geometry, a rectangular shading patch, or a beard line reaching beneath the eyes is a hard rejection.
- Short, cropped, and dense coiled styles normally bind rigidly to the head and do not need secondary simulation. Medium, long, tied-back, and braided styles use the smallest readable chain of hair bones, normally three to six, with damped runtime spring motion plus head, neck, and shoulder collision. Provide a static or reduced-motion distance fallback.
- For the Human pilot specifically, `shaved-buzzed` is the no-module scalp state; `cropped`, `parted`, and `curly-coiled` are exact-scalp rigid modules; and `long`, `tied-back`, and `braided` require accepted secondary-bone motion. This six-module library is shared by Human classes. Class equipment may hide or constrain a style through a separately tested equipment-compatibility rule, but it must never trigger a duplicate class-specific groom.
- Tripo may supply a style reference or quarantined rough starting mesh when a groom is otherwise blocked, preferably using generate-in-parts before retopology. A Tripo hair-bearing character is not a canonical hair module by itself: it must still be separated, exact-head refitted, retopologized/carded, textured, skinned, collision-tested, and receipted. Do not batch paid hair generations merely to avoid the groom/card pipeline.
- Do not integrate a strand renderer such as TressFX into the current Three.js/WebGL runtime. That DirectX/Vulkan-class renderer and simulation stack is a separate engine project, not an asset-pipeline shortcut. A commercial Blender card authoring add-on may accelerate the offline step only after explicit owner approval for purchase/install; its output remains subject to every normal gate.
- Every module must follow the exact scalp, hairline, temple, ear, jaw, lip, neck, and shoulder clearances appropriate to its design. It must preserve a believable hairline and strand, card, or groom breakup under the approved PBR hair material; a glossy helmet, exposed scalp band, floating moustache, detached beard shelf, ear penetration, neck penetration, torso penetration, or visible cap seam is a hard rejection.
- Prove each module from front, both profiles, rear, close-up, and continuous turntable views on the exact head. Then replay the relaxed creator idle, jaw opening, speech visemes, representative expressions, and accepted age/identity extremes. Long styles must also clear the neck and shoulders throughout motion. Prove base tint, extreme light/dark tint, and greying without destroying strand detail.
- Perform this self-review before owner review. Do not ask the owner to inspect a candidate that already fails any visible gate.
- Live owner review supersedes an earlier automated, local, or receipt-authored `PASS`. On rejection, remove the module geometry from the shipped provider asset, record the rejected names and evidence, and keep the creator fail closed until a rebuilt candidate passes a new review. Hiding a rejected module only in the UI is insufficient.
- The reusable production path is: exact-head source -> offline module generation or modeling -> exact-head fit and deformation setup -> multi-angle and motion validation -> fresh import and receipt -> runtime discovery. Never substitute runtime image generation or an unvalidated asset from a merely similar head.

### 6. Strip authoring state and export

Before runtime export:

- bake the accepted shapes and helper motion;
- delete every temporary `META-*` and `RIG-*` object;
- remove Rigify-only drivers, constraints, modifiers, generated bones, and embedded authoring actions;
- export only the canonical head/body assembly, required modular parts, standardized shape keys, materials, and the original body-owned armature; and
- fresh-import the result in a clean Blender process.

The clean import must contain exactly one accepted 65-bone armature with the recorded root and hierarchy. A generated Rigify bone, duplicate skeleton, changed rest transform, or embedded authoring action is a hard failure.

## Fail-closed validation

### Structural gates

- source repository, commit, pack, license, exact target count/names, per-file hashes, and aggregate hash match the receipt;
- neutral `Basis`, UV, material, normal, seam, scale, and skeleton signatures are unchanged;
- each target-derived Rigify placement, landmark, ordered curve, helper, semantic skinning region, control value, corrective, and baked-target hash is receipt-locked;
- no generated module or weight crosses its exact semantic boundary, opposite side, unaffected region, or neck seam;
- the rejected global RBF/similarity artifacts remain quarantined and are never consumed by the direct exact-head builder;
- all neck-seam vertices move no more than `1e-6` in every face, viseme, age, and identity target;
- no NaN/Inf values, unexplained left/right crossover, coordinate-duplicate split, triangle inversion, open tear, or disallowed self-intersection;
- all 52 exact ARKit-compatible names and all 14 non-silent direct Meta-style viseme names exist, while `viseme_sil` remains a zero-weight runtime state; and
- the fresh runtime import contains no authoring rig residue and preserves the accepted 65-bone contract.

### Visual and motion gates

Review neutral plus every target and critical combination at normal speed from front, profile, and three-quarter views. At minimum prove:

- complete clean left, right, and bilateral blink closure without eyeball deformation;
- eight-direction gaze with lids following naturally;
- jaw open/close, left/right, and forward without upper-face drift or detached chin behavior;
- mouth closure, lip separation, pucker, funnel, smile, frown, stretch, press, roll, and cheek/brow motion without tearing;
- teeth remain seated, the tongue stays inside the cavity except `tongueOut`, and speech contacts remain readable;
- speech visemes blend coherently through recorded dialogue rather than isolated stills only;
- age and identity extremes preserve the neck seam, facial controls, and likeness; and
- every hair/facial-hair module clears neutral plus the full accepted creator envelope.

Technical export success, a single still, a contact sheet without continuous playback, or an agent-authored `PASS` label cannot substitute for this review.

## Required per-head receipt

Each head manifest records:

- exact input hashes and accepted neutral evidence;
- Smart Mesh topology/UV/material signatures and neck-seam version;
- exact-head semantic-fit, metarig-placement, and binding schema versions plus SHA-256 values;
- Blender and Rigify versions plus metarig-fit receipt;
- source repository commit, licenses, pack hashes, all target hashes, and target-name sets;
- neutral, semantic-fit, binding, seam, anatomy, combination, stripping, fresh-import, and runtime results;
- age/identity and hair/facial-hair envelope results;
- output GLB/FBX hashes; and
- proof that world, creator, NPC close-up, dialogue, quest, and paper-doll instances resolve the same canonical head asset and controls.

Any source-head topology change invalidates the semantic fit, fitted Rigify rig, binding, every baked/corrected target, age/identity morph proof, modular-fit proof, and runtime facial evidence. Re-run the whole per-head procedure; do not patch around stale fits or weights.
